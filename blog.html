Seeing the world

Posted on November 1, 2025

Before studying abroad, I had never been outside the country. Traveling always seemed exciting, but it was not until I studied abroad in Barcelona that I truly understood how much it could change me. Living in another country for a few months opened my eyes to new cultures, ideas, and ways of living. Barcelona quickly became one of my favorite places. I loved the city’s energy, the food, and the way people enjoyed life at a slower pace. It showed me how important it is to appreciate where you are instead of always rushing to what is next.

The first few weeks were filled with learning and adjustment. I had to figure out how to navigate a new city, use public transportation, and communicate across language barriers. Every challenge came with a sense of accomplishment once I figured it out. The smallest things, like ordering food in Spanish or finding my way home, made me feel more independent. I began to understand what it meant to truly live somewhere instead of just visiting.

While abroad, I traveled to Morocco, Iceland, Switzerland, Florence, Dublin, Lisbon, Budapest, Madrid, Paris, and London. Each place was completely different from the next, and each one taught me something new. In Morocco, I learned how welcoming and kind people can be, even to complete strangers. In Iceland, the natural beauty was unlike anything I had ever seen, reminding me how small we are in such a vast world. Florence taught me to slow down and enjoy art, food, and the company of others. Every trip added something new to how I saw life.

Traveling also taught me flexibility. Plans do not always go the way you expect, and sometimes those moments turn into the best memories. Whether it was missing a train or getting lost in a new city, I learned to stay calm and make the best of it. I found that when I stopped worrying about everything being perfect, I enjoyed myself more. Those experiences showed me how important it is to let go and enjoy the moment.

One of the biggest lessons I took away from studying abroad was independence. Being far from home forced me to rely on myself and make decisions on my own. It gave me confidence and helped me realize how capable I am. I started to trust my instincts more, and I found a new sense of pride in being able to handle things on my own.

What surprised me most was how traveling helped me connect with people from all over the world. Even though everyone came from different backgrounds, we shared the same values of kindness, curiosity, and wanting to make the most out of life. It reminded me that people everywhere have more in common than we think.

Studying abroad taught me more than any classroom ever could. It gave me a global perspective, a better understanding of who I am, and an appreciation for other cultures. I learned that the world is much bigger than I realized, and there is always something new to explore. Traveling has become something that inspires me and motivates me to keep learning.

Looking back, Barcelona will always hold a special place in my heart. It was where I found my love for travel and where I started to see the world differently. Every country, every conversation, and every moment away from home taught me something valuable. Now, traveling is not just something I enjoy — it is something that has become part of who I am and something I plan to continue throughout my life.

Dancing through life

Posted on October 25, 2025

I started dancing when I was four years old, and it has been a part of my life ever since. What began as a fun activity quickly turned into something I truly loved. Over the years, dance became much more than just a hobby; it became a lifestyle. I danced competitively for fifteen years, and during that time, it shaped me into the person I am today. Dance taught me discipline, focus, and what it really means to work hard for something you care about.

When I think back on all those years in the studio, I remember how much time, energy, and dedication it took. We spent nearly forty hours a week practicing, rehearsing, and preparing for competitions. There were days filled with excitement and laughter and others that ended in frustration or tears. But every moment, even the hardest ones, pushed me to become stronger. My coaches were tough but supportive. They expected the best from me and taught me to never settle for less. They helped me understand that success does not come overnight. It comes from showing up, staying consistent, and putting your heart into what you do.

The lessons I learned through dance have carried into every part of my life. I learned how to manage pressure, stay calm under stress, and keep pushing forward even when things do not go as planned. Competitions were always intense, and the nerves before stepping on stage were real, but those moments taught me how to channel fear into focus. I learned how to perform with confidence even when I was unsure of myself. That skill, to keep going no matter what, is something I use in everything I do today.

Dance also taught me how to work as part of a team. In group routines, every dancer had to rely on one another. One person being slightly off could change the entire performance, so communication and trust were everything. I learned how to support others, take direction, and lead when needed. Those lessons have helped me in school, in group projects, and in every collaborative environment I have been a part of.

What I love most about dance is how it allows me to express myself in ways words never could. It is an art form that blends strength and grace, control and emotion. When I dance, I feel free. Every movement tells a story, sometimes joyful, sometimes powerful, and sometimes deeply emotional. It taught me that expression is more than what you say; it is what you feel and how you show it.

Dance built my confidence from a young age. It made me believe in myself and taught me how to hold my head high even when things were difficult. There were many moments when I doubted myself, when I missed a step or fell short in a routine, but those experiences made me stronger. I learned that setbacks do not define you; how you respond to them does.

Looking back, I realize that dance has influenced every part of who I am. It taught me discipline, patience, creativity, and resilience. It showed me that real growth happens outside of your comfort zone. I learned that passion and perseverance can take you further than talent alone.

Even though I no longer dance as intensely as I used to, it will always be a part of me. It shaped my mindset and my confidence. It taught me that dedication pays off and that hard work, no matter the goal, always leads to progress. Dance gave me strength, purpose, and the ability to express myself in a way that words never could. It taught me that with hard work, passion, and belief in yourself, anything is possible, and that lesson will stay with me for life.

Home cooked meals

Posted on October 20, 2025

My love for cooking started pretty recently, but it has quickly become one of my favorite things to do. Growing up, my mom was and still is an amazing cook. Every meal she made brought people together, and I always admired how she made it look so easy while putting so much care into it. From her homemade meatballs and chicken parmesan to her chicken noodle soup, crabcakes, steak, salads, bolognese, and hummus, everything she made had its own special touch. Food was always a big part of our home, and it created memories that I still think about today. Dinner was more than just eating; it was a time for family, laughter, and connection. Those moments made me realize how powerful food can be when it comes to bringing people together.

Even though I grew up surrounded by good food, I never really cooked much myself until I got to college. Once I moved away from home, I realized how much I missed my mom’s cooking. It was not just about the food itself, but about the comfort that came with it. That was what inspired me to start learning her recipes and trying them on my own. At first, I had no idea what I was doing. I would call her constantly while cooking, asking what I was doing wrong or how to fix something that did not turn out quite right. She would always laugh, give me advice, and remind me to be patient. Slowly, I started to understand the rhythm of it, the timing, the flavor, and the small details that make a meal special.

Over time, cooking went from something I was learning to something I truly looked forward to. It became a way to relax and unwind after a long day. I started to love the process itself, from chopping ingredients and hearing the sound of food sizzling in the pan to seeing everything come together into something delicious. Cooking gave me time to slow down, focus, and be creative. It was one of the few things that helped me stay present in the moment, which is something that can be hard to do with everything else going on in life.

There is also something rewarding about creating something from scratch and being able to share it with others. I love the feeling of finishing a meal and knowing I made it myself. It gives me a sense of accomplishment, but it also reminds me of home and the memories that come with it. Whenever I make one of my mom’s recipes, it feels like she is there with me, even from miles away. Those familiar flavors bring back memories of Sunday dinners, family holidays, and simple nights around the table.

Cooking has taught me more than I expected. It has taught me patience, creativity, and problem-solving. It has shown me that not everything goes perfectly the first time, but that you can always learn and improve. Sometimes a recipe does not turn out how you planned, but there is always another chance to try again. I have learned that the best meals often come from experimenting and not being afraid to make mistakes.

What I love most about cooking is that it is always a learning process. There is always a new recipe to try, a new flavor to explore, or a new technique to master. It makes me feel creative, independent, and connected to something familiar at the same time. Cooking gives me a way to express myself and share a piece of who I am with others.

I may not be a professional chef, but cooking has become something that brings me genuine happiness. It connects me to my family, reminds me of home, and gives me space to grow and learn. What started as a way to recreate my mom’s recipes has turned into something that feels like my own. Cooking is not just about making food; it is about creating memories, showing love, and finding joy in the process.
